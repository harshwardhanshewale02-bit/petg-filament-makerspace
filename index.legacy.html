Executive Summary

Turning an ecological challenge into sustainable additive manufacturing material.

🌍

The Environmental Crisis

Billions of single-use Polyethylene Terephthalate (PET) beverage bottles end up in landfills and oceans every year. Our project directly tackles this challenge by repurposing local plastic waste right in our makerspace.

⚙️

The Engineering Solution

Using a precision thermal pultrusion and extrusion mechanism, continuous PET bottle ribbons are drawn through an actively heated brass nozzle, reforming flat plastic strips into continuous cylindrical 1.75mm filament.

💡

Makerspace Philosophy

Built on low-cost, open-source principles. By empowering students, designers, and hobbyists to manufacture their own print filament on-demand, we make 3D printing both economical and zero-waste.

Development Pipeline

From discarded beverage bottle to functional 3D printed prototype.

STAGE 01

Collection & Prep

Collection, thermal label detachment, cleaning, and uniform wall thickness inspection of transparent PET bottles.

STAGE 02

Precision Slitting

Guided continuous ribbon cutter slicing the bottle body into a seamless, uniform ~9mm plastic strip with minimum burr.

STAGE 03

Thermal Pultrusion

Pulling the strip through a PID-controlled heated block (200°C–230°C) with a tapered nozzle to reshape it into 1.75mm solid core.

STAGE 04

Calibrated Spooling

Automated stepper-motor driven puller and spool winder ensuring uniform tension and dimensional tolerance checks.

STAGE 05

Test 3D Printing

Validating layer adhesion, tensile strength, and printing temperature settings on open-source FDM 3D printers.
