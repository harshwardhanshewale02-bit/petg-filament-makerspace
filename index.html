SEC 02

The problem, and what inspired us

In our Makerspace innovation lab at Sanjivani University, we examined the acute paradox between pervasive municipal plastic waste and the high material cost of additive manufacturing.

Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol-modified (PETG) bottles represent one of the world's most ubiquitous plastic container streams. While millions of bottles enter landfills and waterways daily, PETG is an exceptional engineering thermoplastic possessing high tensile strength (55–65 MPa), high thermal resistance, and chemical stability—qualities ideal for additive manufacturing.

Commercial 3D printing filaments cost ₹1,200 to ₹2,200 per kilogram, creating a recurring financial bottleneck for university labs and prototyping workshops. We engineered a small-scale, decentralized pultrusion system that transforms municipal waste bottles directly into high-value engineering feedstocks at near-zero raw material cost.

SEC 03

How it is meant to work

Rather than shredding plastic bottles into chips and melting them through an expensive, power-hungry motorized screw extruder, our project employs continuous thermal pultrusion.

When a continuous PETG ribbon of calibrated width (approx. 9.0 mm) is drawn under linear tension through a conical heated brass die at 250°C, the polymer softens past its glass transition temperature into a viscoelastic state. The 45° internal chamfer inside the brass nozzle radially folds the flat strip upon itself into a dense, solid-core cylindrical profile of nominal 1.75 mm diameter.

Immediately upon exiting the nozzle orifice, a focused cooling air stream quenches the filament below its glass transition temperature (~75°C), locking its circular geometry and preventing sag or ovality before it reaches the geared puller wheels.

Why diameter tolerance matters (±0.05 mm)

FDM 3D printer hotends rely on a calibrated volumetric feed rate: $V = \pi \cdot (d/2)^2 \cdot L$. If filament diameter drifts above 1.85 mm, it jams inside the PTFE Bowden tube or heatbreak. If it falls below 1.65 mm, the extruder drive gear slips, causing under-extrusion and weak, porous layer adhesion. By maintaining continuous pull tension and active cooling, our system holds tolerances within ±0.05 mm.

01

PETG Bottle Collection

Post-consumer clear PETG bottles and containers collected and sorted by wall thickness.

02

Cleaning & Prep

Adhesive labels removed with warm-water soak (50°C); bottles dried to eliminate moisture.

03

PETG Ribbon Slitting

Bearing-guided razor fixture slices continuous 9.0 mm uniform ribbon from bottle wall.

04

Thermal Pultrusion

Ribbon drawn through 250°C brass nozzle; 45° funnel folds strip into 1.75 mm cylinder.

05

Active Quenching

Radial blower duct chills strand below $T_g$, stabilizing diameter without flattening.

06

Spool Winding

High-torque puller motor and synchronized slip-clutch spool wind finished filament.

07

3D Printing

Filament loaded into FDM 3D printer; prints functional prototypes at 255°C nozzle.

    High-resolution datalogging of nozzle thermistor during continuous ribbon drawing to verify that incoming cold strip does not cause thermal sagging below 245°C.

    SEC 09

    Safety and maintenance

    Operating high-temperature heating blocks (250°C) and sharp cutting blades requires rigorous safety controls compliant with Sanjivani University Makerspace laboratory regulations.

    Thermal Hazard Barriers (>230°C)

    Aluminum heating block is enclosed with a high-temperature silicone thermal sock and perforated metal guard, preventing accidental skin burns during operation.

    Mechanical Blade Guarding

    The razor blade in the ribbon slitter is housed within a tight 1.5 mm channel, completely isolating the cutting edge from operator contact.

    Electrical Protection & E-Stop

    Dual inline automotive fuses protect the 24V heater and motor lines; a physical emergency stop button instantly interrupts AC mains power.

    Makerspace Ventilation

    PETG processing below 260°C emits negligible VOCs; operation is restricted to active ventilation bays equipped with overhead extractors.
